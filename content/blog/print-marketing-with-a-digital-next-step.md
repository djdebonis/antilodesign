A flyer catches someone’s attention at an event. A product label gives them a reason to learn more. A business card follows a good conversation. What happens when they pick up their phone?

Print marketing works as part of a larger experience. For a small business, the useful question is how the printed piece helps someone continue that experience, whether they want to ask a question, find a store, or sign up for an event.

## Give the piece one main job

Start with the situation in which someone will see it. A sign needs to communicate quickly from a distance. A handout can offer more explanation. A label must work within the space and shape of the packaging.

Choose the primary action before writing every detail. If the goal is an event registration, make the event, date, location, and registration path easy to find. If it is a service inquiry, explain the service and where it is available. Avoid asking a small piece to carry the entire business story.

## Build the destination alongside the design

A QR code that leads to a general homepage can make the reader start searching again. Choose a destination that continues the promise on the printed piece. A specific event page, product page, or service page gives the next step a clearer context.

The language should match. If the flyer introduces a workshop, the linked page should immediately identify that workshop. If the piece describes a local offer, make the actual area and any relevant conditions understandable on the page.

For an event, practical details such as the venue and arrival instructions may matter more than another paragraph of promotional language. Include what is accurate and useful for that particular setting.

## Treat the QR code as a link people must be able to use

Include a short, readable web address where practical so the QR code is not the only route. Use a destination you expect to maintain, especially for packaging or printed pieces that may remain in circulation.

Print a sample at the final size and test it with more than one phone. Check that it opens the right page, that the page works on mobile, and that the action can be completed. Test again after changes to the destination. A code can scan successfully while leading to an outdated offer.

## Plan for the piece to outlive the promotion

Printed materials can stay on a counter or in a drawer long after an event ends. Decide what the destination should do afterward, before the print run leaves your control.

For a fictional workshop flyer, the path might be:

| Stage | What the destination should explain |
| --- | --- |
| Registration open | The same workshop name, date, venue, availability, and registration action promised on the flyer |
| Fully booked | That registration is closed, with a waitlist only if someone actually manages it |
| Event finished | That this date has passed, with an accurately described next event or a contact option |

Keep that transition useful and honest. Sending an old workshop link silently to a different paid offer can make the printed promise confusing. A maintained page can explain what happened and help the reader choose their next step.

### Assign ownership before approving the proof

Record who controls the destination URL, who can update the page, and who will check it when the offer changes. If the code depends on an outside service, confirm its terms and ongoing costs before committing it to materials. Keep the destination and ownership details with the final artwork so a future team member can maintain the connection.

When reviewing results, distinguish a visit from the action you wanted. A scanned code may show interest; it does not establish registration or attendance. The [measurement article](/blog/marketing-measures-that-matter/) explains how to keep the action and its denominator clear.

## Keep the brand recognizable across both places

Our [Colorado Sauce Company work](/work/colorado-sauce-company/) connected the updated labels with the rebuilt Shopify store and the wider brand story. The website’s store locator also gave people a route to explore purchasing in person.

That connection matters even for a much smaller project. Use the same business name and recognizable visual direction across the piece and destination. Read our guide to [brand consistency online and in print](/blog/consistent-brand-web-and-print/) for a practical starting point.

## Check the production details before committing

Confirm the printer’s specifications, proof the contact information, and verify the quantity and delivery needs. Review the piece at actual size rather than only zoomed in on a screen. Decide who approves the final proof.

Printing, paper, finishes, and distribution are separate decisions from design and should be scoped explicitly. A beautiful file is not yet a finished print run.
