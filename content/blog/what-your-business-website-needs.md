Help visitors understand your business and find their next step before adding more to the page.

## Start with the questions a customer brings

What do you do? Is it for someone like me? What happens next? A useful homepage answers those questions early. A visitor should not have to interpret a clever headline to understand the offer.

## Give each page a job

A service page can explain an offer. An About page can introduce the business. A Contact page can make an inquiry easier. When every page tries to do everything, the important details are harder to find.

## Plan the content before the polish

Write down the main message, supporting details, and next step for each key page. That gives design something meaningful to organize. Photography, color, and typography can then reinforce the story.

## Try it on a phone

Read the page on a small screen. Can you find the service, understand the offer, and reach the contact information without a long search? That is a useful first review of any business website.

## Make room for the person behind the business

The Katalytic Collective’s website project began with a particular goal: to carry something of the experience of being in a room with Katy. We worked through page structure, service language, and copy with attention to both warmth and the substance a school leader would need.

That direction shaped a first website draft. The project remains in progress, with content, photography, pages, and forms still being refined.
